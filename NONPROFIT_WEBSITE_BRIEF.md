# Harborlight Commons: Nonprofit Website Brief

## Organization

**Harborlight Commons** is a fictional community nonprofit that helps neighbors turn underused city lots into welcoming gardens, outdoor classrooms, and places to gather. It works with residents, schools, and local businesses in the fictional city of Bellwether.

Its mission is to make shared green space easy to create and care for, especially in neighborhoods with limited access to parks. Harborlight provides small grants, tools, volunteer support, and practical guidance. Its long-term vision is a network of community-led spaces where people can grow food, learn, and build relationships close to home.

## Audiences and goals

- **Neighbors and community groups:** Find support, understand eligibility, and apply to start a project.
- **Volunteers:** Discover upcoming workdays, choose a role, and sign up.
- **Donors and local businesses:** Understand the impact of contributions and donate or offer in-kind support.
- **Educators and partner organizations:** Explore programs and request a collaboration.
- **Journalists and the wider public:** Learn about the organization, its outcomes, and how to get in touch.

The site should make three actions especially clear: **start a garden**, **volunteer**, and **donate**.

## Core capabilities

- Explain the nonprofit’s mission, approach, service area, and community-led values.
- Show current and completed projects, with photos, location context, partner names, and accessible impact measures.
- Describe programs such as garden-start grants, school growing sessions, tool lending, and neighborhood workdays.
- Let visitors find events and volunteer opportunities, then register interest through a short form.
- Guide applicants through grant eligibility, deadlines, required information, and an application inquiry or form.
- Accept one-time and recurring donations through a secure donation provider, with clear gift amounts and confirmation.
- Collect newsletter subscriptions with consent and a plain explanation of email frequency.
- Provide contact paths for general questions, partnerships, press, and accessibility requests.
- Offer downloadable or printable resources for starting and maintaining a community garden.

## Necessary pages

### 1. Home (`/`)

Introduce Harborlight in one sentence, show the primary actions, and feature a current project or community story. Include a concise impact snapshot, upcoming event, and links to programs and ways to help.

### 2. About (`/about`)

Tell the origin story, mission, values, and service area. Introduce staff and board members, explain how residents shape decisions, and link to annual reports and financial transparency information.

### 3. Programs (`/programs`)

Summarize each offering and who it serves. Provide detail pages or clearly separated sections for:

- **Garden Seed Fund:** Small startup grants and hands-on planning support.
- **Grow Together Schools:** Garden-based learning sessions for educators and students.
- **Shared Tools:** Borrowable tools and basic maintenance supplies.
- **Open Plot Days:** Public volunteer events and seasonal workshops.

Each program should state availability, eligibility, cost (if any), and how to take the next step.

### 4. Projects (`/projects`)

Present a browsable set of gardens and outdoor learning spaces. Each project profile should include its neighborhood, community partners, current status, a short story, and a few meaningful outcomes. Avoid implying that a single metric captures community impact.

### 5. Get Support (`/get-support`)

Help residents decide whether Harborlight can support their idea. Explain eligibility, the application timeline, what information is needed, and how selection works. Include an accessible inquiry or application form and a contact option for people who need help applying.

### 6. Volunteer (`/volunteer`)

Describe volunteer roles, age or supervision requirements, accessibility details, and what to bring. List upcoming events with dates, locations, capacity, and registration. Offer a general interest form for people who do not see a suitable event.

### 7. Donate (`/donate`)

Explain what donations make possible and offer one-time and recurring gifts. Provide secure checkout, tax acknowledgment details, and a clear post-donation confirmation. Include a separate path for business sponsorships and in-kind gifts.

### 8. Stories & Resources (`/stories`)

Publish community stories, seasonal updates, practical gardening guides, and downloadable resources. Keep attribution and image permissions clear, and provide accessible formats where possible.

### 9. Events (`/events`)

List workshops, volunteer days, and community gatherings. Each event page should include date and time, location and transit/access notes, registration details, capacity, and a contact for questions.

### 10. Contact (`/contact`)

Provide a general contact form, email, and mailing address, plus clear routing for partnerships, media, and program questions. State expected response time without promising more than the team can deliver.

### 11. Privacy & Accessibility (`/privacy`, `/accessibility`)

Explain how form and newsletter data is used, how long it is retained, and how visitors can request changes or deletion. State the organization’s accessibility commitment and provide a way to report barriers.

## Shared site elements

- A consistent header with links to Programs, Projects, Events, About, and prominent **Donate** and **Get Support** actions.
- A mobile-friendly navigation menu and a footer with contact details, newsletter signup, social links, privacy, and accessibility links.
- Reusable calls to action that use direct, welcoming language and describe the next step.
- Accessible page titles, keyboard navigation, visible focus states, descriptive image text, captions, and sufficient contrast.
- Clear form labels, helpful validation, confirmation messages, and alternatives to online-only participation where feasible.
- Search engine metadata and social sharing details for public pages and stories.

## Content and tone

Use a warm, practical, neighbor-to-neighbor voice. Center community leadership and specific local work. Avoid charity clichés, inflated impact claims, and language that frames residents as passive recipients. Use fictional people, locations, and results consistently in any prototype content.

## Visual direction

Use the project’s **midnight market** styling direction: warm charcoal canvas, electric lime for key actions, dusty lilac for secondary accents, crisp rules, and occasional editorial serif headings. Keep the interface readable and calm, with generous spacing and a few distinct story or project panels rather than a uniform wall of cards.

## Initial release priorities

For a first version, prioritize Home, About, Programs, Get Support, Volunteer, Donate, Contact, and Privacy. Projects, Events, Stories & Resources, and Accessibility content should follow as the organization prepares approved stories, event information, and program details. Donation processing and form submissions should use established services and be tested before launch.
